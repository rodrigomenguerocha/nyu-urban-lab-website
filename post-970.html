NYT: Colleges Snap Up Unused Office Space in a Slumping Market

March 24, 2024

Impact Development NewsSite Selection

The article discusses how colleges and universities across the United States are capitalizing on the current downturn in the office real estate market by purchasing unused office spaces to expand their campuses or acquire real estate. Sacred Heart University's acquisition of the former General Electric headquarters in Fairfield, Connecticut, serves as an example of this trend. The university converted the office buildings into its new West Campus, accommodating classrooms, student lounges, maker spaces, dance studios, and faculty offices.

Several factors are driving this trend, including the decline in office demand due to remote work trends accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic. With office availability rates soaring, universities see an opportunity to acquire office buildings at discounted prices. However, repurposing office buildings for academic use does not fully address the broader issues within the office real estate market, which continues to struggle with high vacancy rates.

Despite potential challenges in adapting office spaces for academic purposes, universities have been creative in their renovations, often at a fraction of the cost of building from scratch. The acquisitions also offer environmental benefits by utilizing existing structures. While some cities may lose tax revenue as these properties transition from commercial to academic use, others view university acquisitions as a means to revitalize downtown areas and stimulate economic activity.

The article highlights other universities' acquisitions, such as the University of Southern California's purchase of an office building in Washington, D.C., and the University of Louisville receiving a donated building from Humana. While there are challenges in repurposing office spaces for academic use, universities see this trend as an opportunity to expand their campuses, accommodate growing student populations, and enhance their presence in key locations.
