Politico: Biden’s hydrogen bombshell leaves Europe in the dust

October 11, 2023

Impact Development NewsInflation Reduction ActRenewable EnergySustainable Infrastructure

The United States is gaining a competitive edge in the global hydrogen industry, attracting investments that were initially targeted for Europe. This shift is attributed to the clean energy subsidies and incentives that have been introduced as part of President Joe Biden's climate agenda. For instance, the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) implemented a $3-per-kilogram subsidy for green hydrogen and provided substantial financial support to promote the hydrogen sector.

One notable example of this trend is the Norwegian company Nel's decision to establish a nearly $500 million factory in Michigan to manufacture equipment for extracting hydrogen from water. Nel cited several reasons for this choice, including proximity to key partners like General Motors, access to the financial benefits of the IRA, the Biden-era CHIPS and Science Act funding for technology development, and state-level grants for green technology in Michigan. While Europe had initially positioned itself as a leader in clean tech investment, the attractive incentives offered by the United States are drawing companies away. The IRA, in particular, has proven to be a significant factor in this shift. Companies in the hydrogen industry, both within and outside the U.S., are increasingly looking to take advantage of these favorable conditions. The global hydrogen industry is valued at over $155 billion, and the European Union has ambitious plans to produce and import 20 million tons of renewable hydrogen annually by 2030. However, the competitive landscape is evolving, and the United States is now considered a major player in the industry. Despite concerns from European industry leaders that the U.S. subsidies may disadvantage European-based companies, some experts see this competition as a catalyst for innovation and growth in the hydrogen sector. This rivalry between regions could ultimately lead to advancements in hydrogen technology, benefiting the industry on a global scale.
